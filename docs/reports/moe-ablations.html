02 · router regularization

There is a threshold, not a monotone optimum.

Below 0.01, experts collapse and validation suffers. Above it, aggregate balance continues to improve while validation does not.

Why 0.01 remains the defaultZero and 0.001 are plainly insufficient. Both 0.01 and 0.1 solve collapse, but 0.1 applies ten times the auxiliary pressure for no measured validation gain. The 0.03 point also shows why “more uniform” is not a useful scalar objective.
What to replicate nextIf this knob deserves more machine time, compare only 0.01 and 0.1 across seeds. The low-coefficient failure region is already qualitatively settled; a five-point reseed would spend most of its compute reconfirming collapse.
03 · reading the evidence

Controlled where it matters; modest where it does not.

Both studies use the same 8k routed family, dataset contract, batch, base learning rate, horizon, and 16-chip v4 topology. Their claims have different replication strength.

Paired bias comparison

Within each tier, the same seed controls initialization and shuffled data order. Reporting the within-seed delta removes much of the ordinary seed variation. There are three pairs per tier, so small differences remain uncertain.

Single-seed coefficient sweep

All five runs use seed 1350. Large failures at 0 and 0.001 are interpretable; the 0.00057-nat gap between 0.01 and 0.1 is not a winner declaration.

Clean training loss

The logged routed training objective includes α × balance loss. The displayed LM curve removes that term. Final validation is already pure cross-entropy and needs no correction.

Expert symmetry

Expert ordinals have no correspondence across runs. This report intentionally avoids “expert i” comparisons and uses max/min shares and exp(H), which are unchanged by relabeling experts.

Working recipe decision

Keep learned biases and keep router auxiliary coefficient 0.01. The first choice avoids a change with no observed benefit. The second applies enough regularization to prevent collapse without treating perfect load uniformity as the scientific target.
